# Forecast findings (Phase 6)

Updated 2026-11-12 on the frozen dataset `data-freeze-2026-11-11` (the first version, 9 test days, is in [archive/freeze-2026-10-24/](archive/freeze-2026-10-24/)). Numbers: [forecast_results.md](forecast_results.md)
(1 hour ahead), [forecast_results_h3.md](forecast_results_h3.md) (3 hours) and [forecast_results_h6.md](forecast_results_h6.md)
(6 hours), generated by `src/forecast/evaluate.py` (check IDs like **F2** point to tables there).
**Treat these as early results: 27 test days from one autumn month.**

## The question and how it was tested
- **Question:** at the start of an hour, will this station have at least one stockout (no bikes while open for rentals) in the next hour?
- **Honest testing:** for each test day, every model is trained only on earlier days, then predicts that whole day, then we move forward one day (a "rolling-origin" backtest). The first 7 days are training only, which leaves 27 test days (Oct 16 to Nov 11) and 174,686 predictions per model. Stuck and not-renting readings are left out as decided in the charter.
- **Scoring:** the **Brier score** measures how good the predicted probabilities are (0 is perfect; always guessing the base rate of 20.9% scores about 0.17). Lower is better. **AUC** says how well a model ranks risky station-hours above safe ones (0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect).
- **Uncertainty:** intervals come from resampling whole test days. Days that share weather and weekday effects are not independent, so these intervals are probably still too narrow.
- **Models, simplest first:** (1) persistence: "empty now means empty next hour"; (2) station average; (3) station state: each station's own chance of a stockout given whether it is empty now; (4) hour-of-week average per station; (5) gradient-boosted model (GBM) using the current and recent bike levels and the time of day. No model was tuned on the test days.

## Results: 1 hour ahead (F1, F2)
| Model | Brier (95% interval) | AUC |
|---|---|---|
| GBM | 0.082 (0.076 to 0.087) | 0.93 |
| Station state (best simple baseline) | 0.098 (0.092 to 0.104) | 0.86 |
| Persistence | 0.119 (0.110 to 0.127) | 0.81 |
| Hour-of-week average | 0.143 (0.139 to 0.146) | 0.77 |
| Station average | 0.156 (0.150 to 0.163) | 0.67 |

- The GBM beats the best simple baseline by **16.7%** on Brier (interval 15.9% to 17.5%) and beats the hour-of-week average by 43% (interval 40% to 46%). It had the lowest error on each of the 27 test days (F3), and at every time of day (F4).
- Its probabilities are well calibrated: when it says "about 75% chance", stockouts happen about 74% of the time (F5). Persistence is poorly calibrated (calibration error 0.10 versus 0.007 for the GBM) (F1).
- **The weekly-pattern baseline is still weak at 1 hour** (it only edges out the plain station average, 0.143 versus 0.156): early in the backtest each station has few observations per hour of the week. More weeks helped a little, as expected.

## What the model is actually using (F6)
- By far the most important input is the **lowest bike count seen so far this hour**, then whether the station had a stockout this hour, then how fast the count was falling. Time of day and day of week matter very little (F6).
- In plain terms: the forecast is mostly "this station is nearly empty and still dropping", not a learned weekly routine. That is useful and believable, but it is closer to short-term tracking than to long-range prediction.

## Checks that the result is not a leak
- **Placebo (F7):** when each station's inputs are swapped with another station's in the same hour, the AUC falls from 0.94 to 0.53 (near chance) and the Brier score rises from 0.073 to 0.226, worse than guessing the base rate (0.152) (last test day). The model really depends on each station's own recent state.
- **Longer horizons degrade smoothly, as they should:** Brier 0.082 at 1 hour, 0.130 at 3 hours and 0.150 at 6 hours; AUC 0.93, 0.81 and 0.71.
- Code-level tests confirm that changing future data never changes a feature, and that no training label overlaps a test day (`tests/test_forecast.py`).

## How far ahead is it useful?
- **3 hours:** GBM Brier 0.130 versus 0.141 for the best baseline (station state), a 7.9% improvement (interval 6.8% to 9.0%) and 9.0% better than the hour-of-week average (interval 5.3% to 13.5%).
- **6 hours:** the GBM (0.150) is slightly **worse** than the hour-of-week average (0.143): -5.0% with an interval from -8.6% to -0.6%. With 15 days this looked like a tie; with 27 test days the weekly pattern is ahead. The GBM is still 2.7% better than the station-state baseline (interval 1.5% to 4.0%).
- **Meaning for rebalancing:** a truck needs lead time. The strong results are at 1 hour; by 3 to 6 hours the advantage shrinks a lot. This is a limit to say out loud and a reason the simulation should not assume near-perfect foresight.

## Limits and what would change this
- **27 test days**, one autumn month, one holiday (Oct 12) and Spain's clock change (Oct 25) in the window. No weather or season effects are tested.
- **Stockouts shorter than 10 minutes can be missed**, so the target itself is imperfect. Stations stuck for 12+ hours are excluded from scoring, so results do not cover them.
- **No parameter tuning and no weather inputs yet.** Both are possible next steps, but tuning needs more days to avoid fooling ourselves.
- **Re-run at 60 and 90 days.** More weeks should improve the hour-of-week baseline further. At 6 hours a model that combines the GBM with the weekly pattern is an obvious thing to try.
