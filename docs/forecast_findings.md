# Forecast findings (Phase 6)

First real forecast results. Frozen dataset `data-freeze-2026-10-24`. Numbers: [forecast_results.md](forecast_results.md)
(1 hour ahead), [forecast_results_h3.md](forecast_results_h3.md) (3 hours) and [forecast_results_h6.md](forecast_results_h6.md)
(6 hours), generated by `src/forecast/evaluate.py` (check IDs like **F2** point to tables there).
**Treat these as early, narrow results: 9 test days from one autumn month.**

## The question and how it was tested
- **Question:** at the start of an hour, will this station have at least one stockout (no bikes while open for rentals) in the next hour?
- **Honest testing:** for each test day, every model is trained only on earlier days, then predicts that whole day, then we move forward one day (a "rolling-origin" backtest). The first 7 days are training only, which leaves 9 test days (Oct 16 to Oct 24) and 57,906 predictions per model. Stuck and not-renting readings are left out as decided in the charter.
- **Scoring:** the **Brier score** measures how good the predicted probabilities are (0 is perfect; always guessing the base rate of 21.3% scores about 0.17). Lower is better. **AUC** says how well a model ranks risky station-hours above safe ones (0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect).
- **Uncertainty:** intervals come from resampling whole test days. With only 9 days, and days that share weather and weekday effects, these intervals are probably too narrow.
- **Models, simplest first:** (1) persistence: "empty now means empty next hour"; (2) station average; (3) station state: each station's own chance of a stockout given whether it is empty now; (4) hour-of-week average per station; (5) gradient-boosted model (GBM) using the current and recent bike levels and the time of day. No model was tuned on the test days.

## Results: 1 hour ahead (F1, F2)
| Model | Brier (95% interval) | AUC |
|---|---|---|
| GBM | 0.083 (0.069 to 0.095) | 0.93 |
| Station state (best simple baseline) | 0.099 (0.084 to 0.113) | 0.86 |
| Persistence | 0.119 (0.098 to 0.138) | 0.82 |
| Hour-of-week average | 0.149 (0.142 to 0.157) | 0.75 |
| Station average | 0.159 (0.145 to 0.173) | 0.67 |

- The GBM beats the best simple baseline by **16.5%** on Brier (interval 15.2% to 18.3%) and beats the hour-of-week average by 44% (interval 38% to 52%). It had the lowest error on each of the 9 test days (F3), and at every time of day (F4).
- Its probabilities are well calibrated: when it says "about 75% chance", stockouts happen about 74% of the time (F5). Persistence is poorly calibrated (calibration error 0.10 versus 0.006 for the GBM) (F1).
- **The weekly-pattern baseline is weak here** (it only edges out the plain station average): with about a week of training, each station has just one or two observations per hour of the week. This is a data-quantity effect that should improve with more weeks.

## What the model is actually using (F6)
- By far the most important input is the **lowest bike count seen so far this hour**, then whether the station had a stockout this hour, then how fast the count was falling. Time of day and day of week matter very little (F6).
- In plain terms: the forecast is mostly "this station is nearly empty and still dropping", not a learned weekly routine. That is useful and believable, but it is closer to short-term tracking than to long-range prediction.

## Checks that the result is not a leak
- **Placebo (F7):** when each station's inputs are swapped with another station's in the same hour, the AUC falls from 0.92 to 0.51 (chance) and the Brier score rises from 0.079 to 0.209, worse than guessing the base rate (0.144). The model really depends on each station's own recent state.
- **Longer horizons degrade smoothly, as they should:** Brier 0.083 at 1 hour, 0.131 at 3 hours and 0.151 at 6 hours; AUC 0.93, 0.81 and 0.71.
- Code-level tests confirm that changing future data never changes a feature, and that no training label overlaps a test day (`tests/test_forecast.py`).

## How far ahead is it useful?
- **3 hours:** GBM Brier 0.131 versus 0.143 for the best baseline, an 8.2% improvement (interval 6.6% to 10.7%) and 12.4% better than the hour-of-week average (interval 3.9% to 22.4%).
- **6 hours:** the GBM (0.151) is no better than the hour-of-week average (0.150); the difference is within noise (-0.9% with an interval from -9.6% to 8.8%). It is still 3.7% better than the station-state baseline (interval 1.0% to 7.0%).
- **Meaning for rebalancing:** a truck needs lead time. The strong results are at 1 hour; by 3 to 6 hours the advantage shrinks a lot. This is a limit to say out loud and a reason the simulation should not assume near-perfect foresight.

## Limits and what would change this
- **Only 9 test days**, one autumn month, one holiday (Oct 12) in the window, 3 light rainy days. No weather or season effects can be tested.
- **Stockouts shorter than 10 minutes can be missed**, so the target itself is imperfect. Stations stuck for 12+ hours are excluded from scoring, so results do not cover them.
- **No parameter tuning and no weather inputs yet.** Both are possible next steps, but tuning needs more days to avoid fooling ourselves.
- **Re-run at 30 and 60 days.** More weeks should improve the hour-of-week baseline and show whether the GBM edge holds.
