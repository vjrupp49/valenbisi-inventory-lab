# Segmentation findings (Phase 5)

Frozen dataset `data-freeze-2026-10-24`, 15 complete days. Numbers: [segmentation_results.md](segmentation_results.md)
(check IDs like **P3** point to tables there), generated by `src/analysis/segmentation_stability.py`.
All segments are **provisional** until at least 14 usable days exist per station, and these are the first 15.

## What the segments are (plain terms)
- **ABC** ranks stations by how busy they are (average daily bike-count changes, which is a lower bound on real trips). A = the busiest stations that together make up the first 80% of all activity, B = the next 15%, C = the quietest 5%.
- **XYZ** ranks them by how steady that activity is from day to day. X = steady (variation up to 0.5 of the average), Y = moderate (up to 1.0), Z = erratic.
- The cut-off values (80/95 and 0.5/1.0) are textbook defaults, not tuned to Valencia.

## Results
- **The re-implementation is trustworthy:** the Python version used for the stability tests gives exactly the same classes as the dbt model for every station (Basics).
- **Counts:** of 273 stations, 165 are A, 65 are B and 43 are C (P2). By steadiness, 253 are X, 18 are Y, 1 is Z and 1 has no activity (P1).
- **ABC is not very selective here:** it takes 165 of 273 stations (60%) to reach 80% of activity, so activity is spread widely rather than concentrated in a few stations (P2). Compared with a typical inventory ABC, this is unusual and is worth saying plainly.
- **The ranking of stations is very stable:** between the first and second half of the days, the rank correlation of station activity is 0.98, and between odd and even days it is 0.99 (P3). Stations that are busy in one half are busy in the other.
- **ABC classes are fairly stable:** 90% to 93% of stations get the same class from two different halves of the days, with a kappa of 0.83 to 0.87, which is strong agreement (P3). The bootstrap says 84% of stations land in their most common class in at least 90% of resamples, and 94% do so in at least 70% (P5).
- **ABC class borders are sensitive to the cut-offs:** moving the cut-offs to 75/90 changes 15% of stations and 85/97 changes 11% (P4). That is the expected cost of having many stations near the border, and it means a station's A/B label is less informative than its rank.
- **XYZ barely discriminates:** nearly every station is X (253 of 273), so a simple "same class" share looks high (90% to 92%) but kappa is only 0.42 to 0.54, moderate agreement (P3). Moving the cut-offs to 0.4/0.8 changes 11% of stations (P4). With only 15 days, one weekday/weekend cycle also feeds directly into the variation measure.

## Link to service level (descriptive only)
Among A, B and C stations the median headline stockout rate is 9.2%, 13.9% and 15.0%, and the median blockout rate is 6.7%, 1.0% and 0.3% (P6). So the busiest stations are less often empty but more often full, while the quietest are more often empty. This is a pattern in 15 days, not an explanation.

## What we conclude and what we do about it
- **Use ABC rank (the activity ranking) rather than the hard A/B/C labels** when describing stations, and show the label only with a note that borders are soft.
- **Do not lean on XYZ yet.** The textbook cut-offs do not split these stations. Next step: define XYZ from the relative spread (for example top and bottom quartile of variation) once there are more days and a weekday/weekend adjustment, and test that again.
- **Re-run at 30 and 60 days.** The ranking is stable enough to trust now; the labels should be judged again with more data.

## Limits
- Activity is inferred from changes between snapshots and misses out-and-back trips within 10 minutes.
- 15 days, one autumn month, no seasonal variation.
- One holiday (Oct 12) is inside the window and is not treated differently.
