# Service level findings (Phase 4)

Updated 2026-11-12 on the second frozen dataset `data-freeze-2026-11-11`: 33 complete Madrid-local days (Oct 10 to Nov 11).
The first version (15 days, Oct 10 to Oct 24) is kept in [archive/freeze-2026-10-24/](archive/freeze-2026-10-24/); a comparison is in "What changed with more data" below.
Numbers: [service_level_results.md](service_level_results.md), generated by `src/analysis/service_level.py`
(check IDs like **S3** point to tables there). Figures: `docs/figures/`, made by `analysis/plot_service_level.R`.
Anything marked **Hypothesis** is an idea the data is consistent with, not something we showed.

## How to read these numbers
- A **stockout** is a station that is open for rentals but has no bikes. A **blockout** is a station that is accepting returns but has no free docks.
- Rates are the share of 10-minute snapshots a station was in that state. **Headline** rates leave out stations that are not installed, spells when a station was not renting, and readings where the bike count had been frozen for 12 hours or more (decisions in [analysis_charter.md](analysis_charter.md)).
- Stockout rates are **lower bounds**: a stockout shorter than the 10-minute spacing between snapshots can be missed entirely.
- Thirty-three days is about five weekly cycles in one autumn month. Treat every difference below as an early signal, not a settled fact.

## What we measured
- **Coverage:** 22 weekdays, 10 weekend days and 1 public-holiday day (Oct 12), 1,289,150 installed station-snapshots (S1, S2). Of the snapshots where stations were open for rentals, 0.85% were excluded as stuck (S2). Another 2,700 snapshots were not-renting spells (S2).
- **City-wide:** a station is empty 12.85% of the time (headline), with 13.2% if nothing is excluded and 12.3% under the stricter 6-hour rule. It is full 6.3% of the time (headline 6.32%, raw 6.45%, strict 6.21%) (S3).
- **How much the exclusion rule matters:** the stockout rate moves from 11.4% (exclude anything flat 3 hours or more) to 13.2% (exclude nothing). The conclusion "about one in eight snapshots is a stockout" holds under every rule (S4).

## When stations run empty or full (figure: `hourly_service_level.png`)
- **Stockouts peak overnight on weekdays:** a median of 17.2% to 17.4% of stations are empty from 02:00 to 04:00, and again about 15% to 16% in the 09:00 to 11:00 block. They are lowest in the afternoon, around 9.4% to 11.6% from 14:00 to 17:00 (S5).
- **Weekends look different:** the lowest point is about 9.0% empty at 09:00 to 11:00, and the night-time high is 14.7% at 05:00. Afternoon levels are similar to weekdays, about 11% to 13.4% (S5).
- **Blockouts follow a strong daily shape on weekdays:** 14.4% of stations are full at 03:00, falling sharply from 8.1% at 06:00 to 3.7% at 07:00, bottoming at 2.3% at 15:00, and climbing back to 8.3% by 23:00 (S5).
- **Caution:** the shaded bands in the figure (lowest to highest day) are wide. Small gaps between the weekday and weekend lines are not reliable.
- **Hypothesis:** overnight, stations stay in whatever state they ended the evening in, so both "empty" and "full" shares rise. The morning drop in full stations overlaps with commuting and possibly with truck rebalancing, but we have no truck data to separate the two (see audit D9).

## Where: a few stations carry most of the problem (figures: `station_distribution.png`, `stockout_heatmap_top40.png`)
- **Typical station:** empty 10.9% of the time (median), with the least affected tenth at 3.5% or less and the most affected tenth at 25.8% or more. Fullness is more concentrated: the median station is full 3.4% of the time, but the top tenth are full 17.8% or more (S6).
- **Chronic stations:** none of 273 stations is empty at least half the time, and 30 are empty at least a quarter of the time. One station is full at least half the time (station 299, a single eligible snapshot, so not meaningful) and 9 (including that one) are full at least a quarter of the time. One station was never empty (S7).
- **Most often empty:** stations 113 (47.4%), 112 (43.0%), 105 (42.9%), 114 (38.1%) and 15 (34.5%) (S8). Stations 105, 112 and 114 also had hundreds of readings excluded as stuck (1,360, 1,175 and 598), so their true rates are less certain; station 105 would be 54.9% if nothing were excluded (S8).
- **Most often full:** stations 84 (32.2%), 151 (28.6%), 69 (28.5%), 159 (28.1%) and 68 (27.4%), ignoring station 299 (S9).
- **Peak-hour problems are a separate group:** 16 stations are empty less than a quarter of the time overall but at least half the time in some hours, mostly overnight or early morning (for example station 30 at 04:00: 71.7% empty, against 20.5% overall) (S10). These need timing fixes, not more bikes everywhere.

## What changed with more data (15 days to 33 days)
- **Stable:** the overall picture. About one station-snapshot in eight is a stockout (13.4% then, 12.9% now) and about one in sixteen a blockout (6.7% then, 6.3% now). The daily shape is the same: stockouts high overnight and in the late morning on weekdays, lowest in the afternoon; blockouts high overnight and low in the afternoon.
- **Changed:** the extreme stations are less extreme. In the first 15 days one station was empty more than half the time (56.0%); with 33 days the worst is 47.4%, and 30 stations (down from 36) are empty at least a quarter of the time. Weekday overnight stockout peaks are lower (about 17% instead of about 19%). The first weeks were therefore slightly worse than the month as a whole.
- **Lesson:** a two-week window overstated the worst stations. Station-level rankings are more reliable than the exact levels.

## What this does and does not tell us
- **It supports:** stockouts and blockouts are common (about 1 in 8 and 1 in 16 snapshots), strongly time-of-day dependent, and concentrated in identifiable stations. This is the case for treating the network like inventory nodes with different service levels.
- **It does not show:** why stations behave this way, how many trips were lost (we have no demand data), or whether rebalancing helps (no truck data).
- **Holiday:** Oct 12 is one day, so we cannot say anything about holiday effects.
- **Weather and season:** not addressed (one autumn window).
