# Service level findings (Phase 4)

First real results. Frozen dataset `data-freeze-2026-10-24`, 15 complete Madrid-local days (Oct 10 to Oct 24).
Numbers: [service_level_results.md](service_level_results.md), generated by `src/analysis/service_level.py`
(check IDs like **S3** point to tables there). Figures: `docs/figures/`, made by `analysis/plot_service_level.R`.
Anything marked **Hypothesis** is an idea the data is consistent with, not something we showed.

## How to read these numbers
- A **stockout** is a station that is open for rentals but has no bikes. A **blockout** is a station that is accepting returns but has no free docks.
- Rates are the share of 10-minute snapshots a station was in that state. **Headline** rates leave out stations that are not installed, spells when a station was not renting, and readings where the bike count had been frozen for 12 hours or more (decisions in [analysis_charter.md](analysis_charter.md)).
- Stockout rates are **lower bounds**: a stockout shorter than the 10-minute spacing between snapshots can be missed entirely.
- Fifteen days is two weekly cycles in one autumn month. Treat every difference below as an early signal, not a settled fact.

## What we measured
- **Coverage:** 9 weekdays, 5 weekend days and 1 public-holiday day (Oct 12), 585,757 installed station-snapshots (S1, S2). Of the snapshots where stations were open for rentals, 1.1% were excluded as stuck (S2). Another 1,987 snapshots were not-renting spells (S2).
- **City-wide:** a station is empty 13.4% of the time (headline), with 13.9% if nothing is excluded and 12.9% under the stricter 6-hour rule. It is full 6.7% of the time (headline 6.65%, raw 6.7%, strict 6.5%) (S3).
- **How much the exclusion rule matters:** the stockout rate moves from 12.0% (exclude anything flat 3 hours or more) to 13.9% (exclude nothing). The conclusion "about one in eight snapshots is a stockout" holds under every rule (S4).

## When stations run empty or full (figure: `hourly_service_level.png`)
- **Stockouts peak overnight on weekdays:** a median of 18.9% of stations are empty at 02:00, staying above 18.6% from 01:00 to 04:00, and again about 16.7% to 16.9% in the 09:00 to 11:00 block. They are lowest in the afternoon, around 11.0% to 11.5% from 14:00 to 17:00 (S5).
- **Weekends look different:** about 8.2% empty at 08:00 and 09:00, the lowest point, and a night-time high of 15.9% at 04:00. Afternoon levels are similar to weekdays, about 11% to 12.5% (S5).
- **Blockouts follow a strong daily shape on weekdays:** 15.6% of stations are full at 03:00, falling sharply from 10.0% at 06:00 to 4.4% at 07:00, bottoming at 2.4% at 15:00, and climbing back to 9.8% by 23:00 (S5).
- **Caution:** the shaded bands in the figure (lowest to highest day) are wide, and there are only 9 weekdays and 5 weekend days. Small gaps between the weekday and weekend lines are not reliable.
- **Hypothesis:** overnight, stations stay in whatever state they ended the evening in, so both "empty" and "full" shares rise. The morning drop in full stations overlaps with commuting and possibly with truck rebalancing, but we have no truck data to separate the two (see audit D9).

## Where: a few stations carry most of the problem (figures: `station_distribution.png`, `stockout_heatmap_top40.png`)
- **Typical station:** empty 12.1% of the time (median), with the least affected tenth at 2.8% or less and the most affected tenth at 26.8% or more. Fullness is more concentrated: the median station is full 3.5% of the time, but the top tenth are full 19.1% or more (S6).
- **Chronic stations:** 1 of 272 stations is empty at least half the time, and 36 at least a quarter of the time. None is full at least half the time; 15 are full at least a quarter of the time. Three stations were never empty (S7).
- **Most often empty:** stations 105 (56.0%), 112 (48.6%), 113 (44.6%), 114 (40.8%) and 23 (39.0%) (S8). Stations 105, 112 and 114 also had hundreds of readings excluded as stuck (883, 758 and 505 of about 2,150), so their true rates are less certain; station 105 would be 74.1% if nothing were excluded (S8).
- **Most often full:** stations 67 (36.5%), 69 (36.2%), 84 (34.0%), 71 (32.9%) and 159 (32.3%) (S9).
- **Peak-hour problems are a separate group:** 21 stations are empty less than a quarter of the time overall but at least half the time in some hours, mostly overnight or early morning (for example station 30 at 04:00: 77.8% empty, against 19.7% overall) (S10). These need timing fixes, not more bikes everywhere.

## What this does and does not tell us
- **It supports:** stockouts and blockouts are common (about 1 in 8 and 1 in 15 snapshots), strongly time-of-day dependent, and concentrated in identifiable stations. This is the case for treating the network like inventory nodes with different service levels.
- **It does not show:** why stations behave this way, how many trips were lost (we have no demand data), or whether rebalancing helps (no truck data).
- **Holiday:** Oct 12 is one day, so we cannot say anything about holiday effects.
- **Weather and season:** not addressed (3 light rainy days, one month).
